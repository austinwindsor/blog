## Hiking Culture in Taiwan

From quick urban walks to multi-day alpine ascents, hiking is a beloved pastime in Taiwan. Weekends see locals and expats alike crowding mountain trails around Taipei, while more experienced hikers venture into central Taiwan's national parks.

### Main Hiking Regions in Taiwan

- **Taipei & New Taipei City:** Accessible trails like Elephant Mountain, Teapot Mountain, and Yangmingshan make this area perfect for day hikes with incredible city views and geothermal landscapes.

- **Hualien & Taroko National Park:** Home to dramatic marble gorges and lush canyons. Trails like Zhuilu Old Trail and Lushui-Wenshan showcase Taiwan’s unique geological diversity.

- **Central Mountain Range (Nantou, Taichung, Hsinchu):** The heart of Taiwan's alpine hiking scene, featuring iconic peaks like Hehuanshan, Xueshan, and Yushan (Jade Mountain). Multi-day and permit-based hikes start here.

- **Yilan & Northeast Coast:** Known for coastal cliffs and forested trails that offer a blend of sea and mountain views. Good for intermediate-level hikers.

- **Southern Taiwan (Kaohsiung, Taitung):** Warmer weather and wilder terrain. Explore forest parks, aboriginal trails, and river tracing routes in more remote areas.

## What to Bring on a Hike

**Essentials:**
- Comfortable hiking shoes
- Daypack
- Water (1.5–2L minimum)
- Snacks/lunch
- Sunscreen & bug spray
- Rain jacket

**Optional:**
- Trekking poles
- GPS or hiking app
- Headlamp
- Extra layers

## Best Seasons and Weather Tips

- **Best months:** October–April (cool, dry)
- **Avoid:** Typhoon season (July–September), and days with heavy rain
- **Ideal conditions:** Clear skies, low humidity, moderate temps

## Hiking Safety Tips

- **Check the weather** before you go; mountain weather changes fast  
- **Download offline maps** (Maps.me or Gaia GPS recommended)  
- **Tell someone** where you're going if hiking alone  
- **Start early** to avoid afternoon storms and catch better views  
- **Respect nature** – pack out what you pack in
